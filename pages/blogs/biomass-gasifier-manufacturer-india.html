How to Choose a Biomass Gasifier Manufacturer in India: 10 Questions to Ask

Choose a biomass gasifier manufacturer who will test your own fuel, size the system to your real load, show you working installations, train your operators and keep spares in stock. The machine's price matters less than how well it runs on your biomass for the next ten years.

By Rai Singh Dahiya, Founder, Enersol Biopower  |  First published 29 September 2025  |  Updated 3 November 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Ask the manufacturer to test the gasifier on your own biomass before you buy.
  • Size the system by your actual running load, not by the biggest number on a brochure.
  • Visit at least one working installation and speak to its operator.
  • Check what the price includes: installation, commissioning, training and spares.
  • Judge the offer by cost per unit of power over its life, not by the purchase price alone.

Why the Manufacturer Matters More Than the Machine

A biomass gasifier turns solid biomass such as rice husk, wood chips or crop residue into producer gas, which then runs an engine for electricity or a burner for heat. On paper, most gasifiers look alike. In practice, two systems of the same rating can perform very differently on the same fuel.

The difference comes from design choices that only an experienced manufacturer gets right: matching the reactor to the fuel, cleaning the gas well enough to protect the engine, and supporting the plant after it is installed. A poor choice usually shows up months later as tar in the engine, frequent shutdowns and spare parts that take weeks to arrive.

India's biomass power potential is large. The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) puts it at over 25 GW, much of it still untapped. That makes it worth choosing a supplier carefully, because a well-chosen gasifier can replace diesel or grid power for many years.

10 Questions to Ask Any Biomass Gasifier Manufacturer

1. Will you test the gasifier on my biomass?

Every fuel behaves differently. Rice husk, wood chips, coconut shells and crop residue vary in size, moisture and ash. A good manufacturer will ask for a sample of your fuel and tell you how the gasifier will perform on it. Be careful with any supplier who says one design works with every fuel without checking.

2. Do I need power, heat, or both?

Power gasifiers feed producer gas to an engine and are chosen by kW. Thermal gasifiers feed a burner, dryer or furnace and are chosen by heat output. Some sites need both. A manufacturer should ask about your application before quoting, not after. For thermal use, see our guide to biomass gasifiers for process heat.

3. How did you work out the size?

An oversized gasifier runs inefficiently at part load, and an undersized one is overloaded every day. Ask the supplier to show how they calculated the capacity from your running load, the starting load of your motors and your daily operating hours. A clear answer is a sign of real engineering.

4. How is the gas cleaned before the engine?

Tar and dust in producer gas are the most common reason engines wear out early. Ask what cooling, filtering and cleaning stages the system has, how often filters need attention, and what happens to the waste water or residue they produce.

5. Are the engine and alternator matched to producer gas?

Producer gas has less energy than diesel, so the engine must be set up for it. Ask who supplies the engine and alternator, whether they are matched to the gasifier, and who services them. One supplier responsible for the whole system is easier to deal with than two who blame each other.

6. Can I see a working installation?

Brochures are easy to print. Running plants are not. Ask for references from customers with a similar fuel and load, and visit at least one site. Speak to the operator, not just the owner, about breakdowns, spares and support.

7. What recognition and track record do you have?

Years in business, independent awards and work with research institutions show that a design has been tested outside the factory. Ask how long the company has been building gasifiers and where its systems are running today.

8. Are installation, commissioning and training included?

A gasifier is only as good as the person running it. Confirm that the quote covers installation, commissioning and hands-on operator training at your site, and ask whether written operating and maintenance instructions are provided.

9. How fast can you supply spares and service?

Ask which wear parts are needed and how often, whether they are kept in stock, and how quickly an engineer can reach you. For a plant that replaces diesel, every day of downtime costs money.

10. What will each unit of power really cost me?

Compare offers on lifetime cost, not purchase price. Ask for the expected biomass use per kWh at your load, the maintenance schedule and its cost, and the expected life of the main parts. A cheaper gasifier that uses more fuel or breaks down often can cost more within a year or two.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose the right biomass gasifier manufacturer in India?

Choose one who tests your own fuel, explains how they sized the system, lets you visit a working installation, and includes installation, operator training and spares support in the offer.

What should I ask before buying a biomass gasifier?

Ask whether it has been tested on your biomass, how the size was calculated, how the gas is cleaned, whether the engine is matched to producer gas, and what each unit of power will cost over the system's life.

Can one biomass gasifier run on any fuel?

No. Rice husk, wood chips and crop residue differ in size, moisture and ash, so a gasifier should be designed or adjusted for the fuel you will actually use. Ask the manufacturer to check a sample first.

Is the cheapest biomass gasifier a good choice?

Not always. Fuel use, maintenance and downtime decide the real cost. Compare offers on the cost of each unit of power over several years, not on the purchase price alone.

What support should a gasifier manufacturer provide after installation?

Commissioning at your site, hands-on operator training, written maintenance instructions, spare parts in stock and a clear service response time.

Biomass Stoves for Caterers: Cooking for Weddings and Large Events

Caterers can use biomass stoves for bulk dishes such as dal, rice, curries, tea and milk, and cut their dependence on LPG cylinders. The keys are to match each stove to the job, plan fuel for every event, and set up in a safe, well-ventilated area away from guests and tents.

By Rai Singh Dahiya, Founder, Enersol Biopower  |  Published 12 October 2026

Key Article Highlights

  • Start with long-boiling dishes, and keep LPG for delicate work at first
  • Weight matters, so choose stoves that your team can really transport
  • Plan fuel for each event, then add a 20% reserve
  • Forced-draft stoves need power, so plan your generator and cables
  • Keep the cooking area away from tents, decorations and guests

Why Caterers Are Looking Beyond LPG

Catering is a tight business. You often quote a price weeks before the event, so a rise in LPG prices comes straight out of your margin. Commercial LPG reached about ₹2,078 a cylinder in April 2026, and a large event can use several cylinders. In the wedding season, getting enough of them on time can be a problem in itself.

Biomass gives you another option. Fuel such as briquettes can be bought in advance, stacked and carried to the venue. You can also set your fuel cost before you quote. It does need more planning than a gas connection, and this guide shows you where to start.

How to Plan Fuel for Each Event

Treat fuel like any other event material. In this illustrative example, three stoves run for 5 hours at about 8 kg an hour each.

3 stoves × 8 kg × 5 hours = 120 kg
120 kg + 20% reserve = about 144 kg

Weigh what you actually use at your first few events, and keep a record by menu and guest count. You will soon be able to work out your fuel per 100 guests, and add it to your quotations.

  • Choose one fuel: Briquettes or pellets are usually easier to pack and feed than loose fuel.
  • Keep it dry: Stoves need fuel below 20% moisture, so carry it in covered bags and keep it off wet ground.
  • Buy before the season: Book your fuel before wedding dates pile up.
  • Check the cost: Compare your fuel cost per event with what you spent on LPG.

Setting Up at the Venue

Visit the venue before the event, if you can, and agree where the cooking area will be.

1
Get permission: Tell the venue that you will use biomass stoves, and follow its rules.
2
Choose the place: Pick open or well-ventilated ground, with wind blowing away from guests.
3
Level the stoves: Set them on firm, flat, non-flammable ground. Check that they stay stable with a full vessel.
4
Connect the power: Run fans and blowers from a safe supply, and protect cables from heat and footfall. Keep a backup source ready.
5
Start early: Allow time for lighting and for any changes. Your first events will need more time.

Event Safety Essentials

  • Clear space: Keep stoves away from tents, cloth, decorations, dry grass and fuel stacks.
  • Keep people back: Rope off the cooking area from guests and children.
  • Fire equipment: Keep suitable extinguishers within reach, and tell your team who does what.
  • Hot ash: Put it in a covered metal container. Never leave it in the open or in a bag.
  • Hot stoves: Let them cool fully before you move or load them.
  • Local rules: Follow your local fire safety rules and venue requirements.

Getting Your Team Ready

Your karigars have years of experience with gas, and they are the ones who will make this work. Bring them in early.

  • Practise first: Try the stoves in your base kitchen before any client event.
  • Start small: Use them first at a small event, with LPG ready as backup.
  • Name a stove lead: Make one person responsible for fuel, fire and ash at each event.
  • Learn the regulator: Fan speed takes the place of the gas knob, and it needs practice.
  • Care for the stoves: Rough transport damages equipment, so clean and check it after each event. Our stove daily care guide can help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can biomass stoves be used for wedding catering?

Yes, they can suit bulk dishes such as dal, rice, curries, tea and milk. They work best at outdoor or well-ventilated cooking areas. Keep the stoves away from tents and guests, and run a trial at a smaller event first.

Which biomass stove is best for a caterer?

It depends on how far you move it and what you cook. Lighter models, such as the 14 kg Bio-Mini or the 40–50 kg Green-Agnika10, are easier to transport. Heavier stoves, such as the 190–200 kg Green-Magic15, suit a fixed base kitchen or a venue with lifting help.

How much biomass fuel do I need for an event?

Multiply the number of stoves by their fuel use per hour and the cooking hours, then add a reserve. Three stoves using 8 kg an hour for 5 hours need 120 kg, or about 144 kg with a 20% reserve.

Do biomass stoves need electricity at an event?

Yes. Forced-draft stoves need power for a fan or blower. Larger models use blowers of about 100–200W, so a small generator or inverter connection can run them. Confirm your power source and safe cabling before the event.

Can I replace all my LPG cylinders with biomass stoves?

Start with long-boiling, high-volume dishes and keep LPG for dishes that need fast or fine control. Once your team is confident, you can move more of your menu across. Many caterers may prefer to run both.

What safety steps should caterers follow with biomass stoves?

Set stoves on flat, non-flammable ground, away from tents, decorations, guests and fuel stacks. Keep a suitable extinguisher nearby, and cool hot ash in a covered metal container. Get venue permission, and follow your local fire safety rules.

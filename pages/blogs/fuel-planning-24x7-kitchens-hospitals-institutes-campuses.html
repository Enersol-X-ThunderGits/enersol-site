Fuel Planning for 24x7 Kitchens in Hospitals, Institutes and Large Campuses

A 24x7 kitchen needs a fuel plan built around its busiest day and its worst day. Work out your daily demand, hold enough buffer stock to cover a late delivery, store it safely, and keep a backup ready for a stove fault, a fuel shortage or a power cut.

By Rai Singh Dahiya, Founder, Enersol Biopower  |  Published 27 October 2026

Key Article Highlights

  • Build your daily fuel demand session by session, then confirm it by weighing
  • Set a reorder level from your supplier's lead time plus safety days
  • Keep fuel dry and below 20% moisture, and use the oldest stock first
  • Plan for stove faults, fuel shortages and power cuts separately
  • In hospitals, keep fuel and flue gases well away from oxygen stores and wards

Why a 24x7 Kitchen Is Different

A school kitchen can close for the evening. A hospital, a residential institute or a large campus often cannot. Patients, students, staff and shift workers may need food early in the morning and late at night.

That leaves little room for error. There is no quiet day to clean a stove, and no spare time if a truck is late. Patient diets, in particular, cannot simply be skipped.

The good news is that a biomass kitchen does not depend on a single cylinder delivery. Once you have a good stock and a good routine, it can be a very steady system. It just has to be planned like the rest of your supply chain.

Step 1: Work Out Your Daily Fuel Demand

Do not guess from a brochure figure. Map your full kitchen day, session by session. This illustrative example shows a large campus kitchen. Replace it with your own numbers.

Example daily biomass fuel demand for a 24x7 kitchen
Session Stoves Fuel per Stove Hours Fuel Used
Breakfast115 kg/hr230 kg
Lunch215 kg/hr390 kg
Dinner215 kg/hr390 kg
Night service1 small7 kg/hr321 kg
Total a day231 kg

That is about 7 tonnes a month. Next, weigh the fuel you actually use for two weeks and correct your figure. Plan for your busiest days too, such as admissions, exams, events or a high patient count, not just an average day.

Step 2: Set Your Buffer Stock

Treat fuel the way you treat rice or medicine, with a clear minimum level. Your reorder level is the stock at which you must place the next order.

Reorder level = daily use × (lead time + safety days)

Say your supplier takes 3 days to deliver, and you want 5 safety days.

231 kg × (3 + 5) days = about 1.85 tonnes

Safety days are your own decision. Add more if your supplier is far away, if roads close in the monsoon, or if the harvest season changes prices and supply. Fill your store before the monsoon starts, because wet fuel is often harder to find just when you need it most.

Step 3: Secure a Reliable Supply

One supplier is a single point of failure. Biomass often comes from small local businesses, so a written agreement helps both sides.

What to Put in a Fuel Agreement

  • Two sources: Name a main supplier and a backup.
  • Fuel type: State the type, size and maximum moisture. Your stove needs fuel below 20% moisture.
  • Quantity and schedule: Agree monthly quantities and delivery days.
  • Quality check: Weigh each load and test moisture at the gate. Reject wet or dirty loads.
  • Price terms: Agree how the price can change through the year.
  • Emergency order: Agree how fast the supplier must respond in an emergency.

Step 4: Plan Your Fuel Storage

Your store must hold your reorder stock plus a full delivery. If you take a weekly delivery of about 1.6 tonnes, plan for about 3.5 tonnes at peak. How much room that takes depends on your fuel, so measure the bulk density of what you buy.

3,500 kg ÷ 400 kg/m³ = about 9 m³, plus walking and loading space
  • Dry and raised: Keep fuel under cover and off the floor, away from rain and floor washing.
  • First in, first out: Use older stock first, and mark each delivery date.
  • A day store: Keep only a small, measured quantity near the stoves, and fill it each shift.
  • A separate route: Keep fuel and ash traffic away from food preparation and serving areas.
  • Pest control: Keep the store clean, and keep it inside your pest control plan.

Step 5: Plan for Three Kinds of Failure

Ask yourself one question for each: if this fails at 2 am, what does the night cook do?

Backup plans for 24x7 kitchen failures
Failure Your Backup
Stove faultOne more stove than peak load needs, plus spare fans and common parts
Fuel shortage or wet fuelBuffer stock, a second supplier, and a reserve for essential meals such as LPG
Power cutConnect fans and blowers to the backup supply. Two 165W blowers need 330W for as long as the cut lasts

Write the plan down, display it in the kitchen, and rehearse it. For hospitals, list the essential meals and special diets that must go out, whatever happens.

Step 6: Build In Safety and Air Quality

Hospitals and residential campuses have people who sleep, recover or study near the kitchen. Get your layout reviewed by your fire safety officer and check local rules before you install.

Safety Essentials

  • Medical gases: In hospitals, keep the kitchen, fuel store and ash well away from oxygen and other medical gas stores or lines.
  • Chimney route: Discharge away from wards, classrooms, hostels, windows and air intakes.
  • Fuel store: Keep it away from stoves, hot ash and electrical panels.
  • Hot ash: Use a covered metal container, and leave it to cool in a safe, set place.
  • Fire equipment: Keep suitable extinguishers in reach, and train every shift to use them.
  • Noise: Check blower noise at night if the kitchen is near sleeping areas.
  • Ventilation: Keep good airflow for staff, and make sure that exhaust systems are working.

Step 7: Run It by Shift

A 24x7 kitchen is run by several teams, so the plan must survive a shift change.

  • Handover log: Record fuel used, stock left, stove condition and any problems at the end of every shift.
  • Trained cover: Train night and relief staff as well as your head cook.
  • Rotating maintenance: Take stoves out of use in turn for cleaning during quiet hours.
  • Ash routine: Set clear times for ash removal in each shift.
  • A simple measure: Track fuel used per 100 meals. A sudden rise can point to wet fuel, a stove problem or waste.

Review the numbers once a month. Compare them with your plan, and update your reorder level as patient numbers, student numbers or menus change. If you are still choosing equipment, use our biomass stove purchase checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much biomass fuel does a 24x7 kitchen need?

List each cooking session, the stoves it uses, their fuel use per hour and the hours they run, then add it up. In our example, a kitchen uses about 231 kg a day, or about 7 tonnes a month. Weigh your fuel for two weeks to find your real figure.

How much buffer stock should a large kitchen keep?

Keep enough for your supplier's lead time plus safety days. A kitchen using 231 kg a day, with a 3-day lead time and 5 safety days, should reorder when stock falls to about 1.85 tonnes. Increase the safety days before the monsoon.

What backup does a hospital kitchen need with a biomass stove?

Plan for three failures: a stove fault, a fuel shortage and a power cut. A spare stove, a second fuel supplier, backup power for the fans and blowers, and a small LPG or other reserve for critical meals will cover most cases.

How should biomass fuel be stored in a campus kitchen?

Keep it dry, covered, raised off the floor and away from stoves, hot ash and electrical panels. Use the oldest stock first, and keep fuel moisture below 20%. Have your storage reviewed under your local fire safety rules.

Can a biomass stove be used safely in a hospital kitchen?

It needs careful planning. Keep the kitchen and fuel store well away from medical gas and oxygen stores, and route the chimney away from wards, windows and air intakes. Get your design reviewed by your fire safety officer and hospital management before you install.

How do you clean stoves if a kitchen never stops?

Keep at least one more stove than your peak load needs, then take stoves out of use in turn for cleaning and inspection during quiet hours. Schedule ash removal for each shift, and record it in a handover log.
